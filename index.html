University of Greenwich · Student Society

Finance, law and the technology now running through both.

FLaT is a society for students who want to understand where financial services and legal practice are actually heading, not just where they've been.

We look at the two industries together, because increasingly the interesting problems sit at the point where they overlap: how regulation responds to AI, how contracts change when they're smart, what happens to legal practice when AI can read case law faster than humans.

Join FLaT See what we do

Most students sign a phone contract, take out a credit card, or accept a job offer with a restrictive clause buried in it, before anyone's taught them what any of that actually commits them to. Millions of adults in the UK are still effectively locked out of mainstream credit and banking, and that's not usually down to bad decisions, it's down to never having had the chance to learn the basics. School doesn't cover it, and by the time it matters, you're expected to have picked it up somewhere.

That gap is getting harder to ignore because AI is now moving through finance and law at the same time, and regulators are struggling to keep up with either sector on its own, let alone both together. Algorithmic lending decisions, AI-drafted contracts and automated legal advice are already live, but the rules governing them are still being written, tested in courts, or not written at all. Understanding how credit, contracts and regulation actually work is no longer just useful background, it's what stops you signing away something you didn't understand, in a system that's changing faster than the safeguards around it.

FLaT exists because that understanding shouldn't depend on having the right background or the right contacts. We cover finance and law together, and the technology moving through both, because that's where the decisions students are already making, often without realising it, actually sit.

What we cover

Three articles, one overlap

§ Article I

How fintech, algorithmic trading and digital assets are changing what a career in financial services actually looks like. We cover open banking, RegTech and the shift toward AI-driven advisory.

§ Article II

How legal practice is adapting to automation, AI governance and the regulatory questions that come with it. We look at legaltech tools, data protection and how firms are having to rethink contract review and due diligence.

§ Article III

Not everyone in the room is aiming for a career in finance or law, and that's fine. This strand covers the basics that are useful regardless of where you end up: how contracts work, what financial regulation actually tries to do, and where the two meet.

What actually happens at FLaT

A mix of formats, most of them open to non-members, all of them free.

FormatWhat it involves
Speaker sessions Practitioners from finance, law and technology talk about their actual day-to-day work, not just the highlights reel.
Workshops Smaller, practical sessions: case study work, mock negotiations, or building a simple financial model from scratch.
Literacy drop-ins Short, open sessions on a single practical topic. No prior knowledge needed and no obligation to join.
Employer contact Where we can arrange it, direct access to people currently working across the sectors we cover.

Why it exists

"I started FLaT because a lot of good, interesting content in finance and fintech assumes a level of access or background that not everyone has. It's meant to be a way in, without gatekeeping."

Madison Farrell — Co-Founder & Co-President, FLaT Society

"My side of FLaT grew out of the same gap, but on the legal and legaltech side of things. Law can feel closed off if you don't already know someone in it, and I wanted a space to change that."

Poulome Yadav — Co-Founder & Co-President, FLaT Society

Vice President
